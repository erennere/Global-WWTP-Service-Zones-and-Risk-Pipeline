\subsection*{Plant size in people}

The service area of a plant grows with the number of people it serves, its size $S_i$.
For \calPlantsReported{} of our \calPlantsAll{} plants a size is reported. We took the first positive
value of the HydroWASTE population served\cite{hydrowaste} (excluding records it estimated from the
population within 11\,km of the plant, quality flag 4), the US Clean Watersheds Needs Survey population
(2022)\cite{cwns2022} and the load entering the plant in the European Urban Waste Water Treatment
Directive data (population equivalents)\cite{uwwtd}. All other plants get a size estimated from the tanks detected on the image.

The detected tank area of plant $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  F_i = \sum_{j} \pi \left(\frac{d_{ij}}{2}\right)^{2} + \sum_{j} A_{ij},
  \label{eq:tank-area}
\end{equation}
where $d_{ij}$ are the diameters of its circular tanks and $A_{ij}$ the areas of its rectangular tanks
(m$^2$). A plant has a usable area when $F_i>0$ and no lagoon was detected (\calPlantsSized{} plants).
We fitted
\begin{equation}
  \log_{10} \hat{P}_i = \alpha + \delta_c + \beta \log_{10} F_i
  \label{eq:size}
\end{equation}
by least squares on the \calFitPlants{} plants with both a usable area and a reported size, first
without $\delta_c$: $\alpha=\calFitIntercept$, $\beta=\calFitSlope$ ($R^2=\calFitRsq$,
Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-plant-size}a). Plants of the same tank area serve more people in some
countries than in others. The country term $\delta_c$ is the median residual
$\log_{10} R_i - (\alpha + \beta \log_{10} F_i)$ over the plants of country $c$ with a reported size
$R_i$. It is used where it rests on at least \calOffsetMinPlants{} plants (\calOffsetCountries{}
countries, a factor $10^{\delta_c}$ between \calOffsetMin{} and \calOffsetMax{}; Extended Data
Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-plant-size}b) and is 0 elsewhere. On these countries the typical error of the estimate
(median $|\log_{10} R_i/\hat P_i|$, as a factor) fell from \calEstimateErrorBefore{} to
\calEstimateErrorAfter.

Plants without a usable area (lagoons, plants without detected tanks) get a fixed size $U_c$: the
median reported size of such plants in country $c$ where there are at least \calOffsetMinPlants{}
(\calUnsizedOwn{} countries); otherwise \calUnsizedRatio{} times the median estimated size of the
country's plants with tanks, the median of this ratio over those countries
(\calUnsizedRatioCountries{} countries); and \calUnsizedDefault{} people where neither is available.

The size before the national scale is $S^{0}_i = R_i$ where a size is reported, $\hat P_i$ where the
plant has a usable area and $U_c$ otherwise. The national scale makes the sizes of a country add up
to the number of people connected to treatment there:
\begin{equation}
  k_c = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{C_c N_c}{\sum_{i \in c} S^{0}_i},\ k_{\min},\ k_{\max}\right),
  \qquad S_i = k_c S^{0}_i ,
  \label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
where $C_c$ is the share of the population whose sewage is collected by a sewer and treated (WHO/UNICEF
JMP, safely managed sanitation via sewer connections, \texttt{san\_sew\_sm\_t}, year nearest
\calYear)\cite{jmp2025} and $N_c$ the WorldPop \calYear{} population of the country\cite{worldpop}.
Without limits, $k_c$ ranges from \calScaleRawMin{} to \calScaleRawMax{} (countries with $C_c>0$; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-plant-size}c).
Values above 1 mean that our plant list misses plants or underestimates their sizes; values below 1
mean that the sizes add up to more people than are counted as connected to treatment, for example where
reported loads include industry or where few sewer connections are counted as safely managed. The limits $[k_{\min}, k_{\max}]$ were chosen with the plant-level
check below (chosen: \calScaleChosen).

\subsection*{Service radius}

A plant serves the people within a circle of radius
\begin{equation}
  r_i = \min\!\left(r_{\max},\ r_{10k,c} \left(\frac{S_i}{10^{4}}\right)^{e}\right),
  \label{eq:radius}
\end{equation}
with $e=\calRadiusExponent$ and $r_{\max}=\calMaxBufferKm$\,km. The radius of a plant of
10{,}000 people, $r_{10k,c}$, was previously one value for all countries (\calFixedRadius\,km,
calibrated on US sewersheds). Here it follows the national connection rate:
\begin{equation}
  r_{10k,c} = \min\!\left(r_{\mathrm{cap}},\ \exp\!\left(a + b \operatorname{logit} C_c\right)\right),
  \label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
with $C_c$ clipped to 1--99\,\%. Countries without a JMP value keep \calFixedRadius\,km.

\paragraph{Stand-in for the service areas.} Calibrating on the full service-area pipeline would take
days per setting, so we used a fast stand-in that keeps its two main elements. The WorldPop population
was summed to \calCellKm\,km cells. Each cell goes to the plant of its country with the smallest
distance divided by $\sqrt{S_i}$ among its \calCandidatePlants{} nearest plants, and it counts as
served when it lies within that plant's radius (equation~\eqref{eq:radius}). Because $k_c$ multiplies
all sizes of a country, it does not change which plant a cell goes to, and it multiplies every radius
of the country by $k_c^{e}$.

\paragraph{Calibration on national statistics.} For each of \calCurveCountries{} countries with plants
and a population raster we computed the served share $s_c(r)$ for \calGridCount{} values of $r_{10k}$
between \calGridStart{} and \calGridStop\,km (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-service-radius}a). The
radius a country needs is the $r^{*}_c$ with $s_c(r^{*}_c)=C_c$; with the national scale it is
$r^{*}_c/k_c^{e}$. We fitted $a$ and $b$ by least squares of $\ln \min(r^{*}_c/k_c^{e},\ r_{\mathrm{cap}})$
on $\operatorname{logit} C_c$ over the \calCountries{} countries with a JMP value, leaving out the few
whose curve does not reach $C_c$ within the tried radii (Extended Data
Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-service-radius}b). To test the rule on countries it was not fitted to, we refitted it
\calRegions{} times, each time without one SDG region, and predicted the served share of the left-out
region.
We did this for each of \calCapCount{} caps (\calCapLow--\calCapHigh\,km, the largest meaning no cap)
and \calLimitCount{} pairs of scale limits (\calLimitList).

The served share was compared with three national statistics that were not used in the fit: SDG
indicator 6.3.1 (household wastewater in sewers divided by all household wastewater, times the share
of the sewer flow delivered to treatment)\cite{sdg631}, Eurostat (population connected to urban and
other wastewater treatment)\cite{eurostat_wwcon} and OECD (public wastewater treatment)\cite{oecd_treat}.

\paragraph{Check on real catchments and choice.} National totals cannot tell whether the people a
plant serves are the right ones. We therefore also ran every setting on datasets of real catchments:
EPA sewersheds of the United States (\calUnitsUsSewersheds{} sewersheds matched to our plants by their
CWNS identifier)\cite{epa_sewersheds}, sewage catchments of Great Britain
(\calUnitsGbCatchments)\cite{gb_catchments}, inhabitants per plant of the Netherlands (CBS,
\calUnitsNlCbs{} plants)\cite{cbs_rwzi} and purification units (\calUnitsNlUnits)\cite{nl_units}, and
agglomerations of Ireland (EPA, \calUnitsIeAgglomerations)\cite{ie_agglomerations}. A polygon was
matched to our plants within \calMatchDistance\,m. Except for the CBS counts, the reference is the
WorldPop population inside the polygon, so the check tests the shape of the service areas, not the
population data. The error of a reference is class-balanced: the median of
$|\log_{10}(\text{predicted}/\text{reference})|$ within three size classes (below $10^4$, $10^4$--$10^5$
and above $10^5$ people), averaged over the classes, so that the many small plants do not dominate.
Its antilogarithm is the typical factor between predicted and reference people. We averaged it over the
references of a country and then over the countries.

The per-plant errors of the \calConfigurations{} settings differ little (typical factor
\calPlantErrLow--\calPlantErrHigh; fixed radius \calPlantErrFixed), less than the noise of the
reference data. To avoid choosing by chance, we drew \calBootstrapSamples{} bootstrap samples of the
catchments (with replacement within each size class, the same draw for every setting) and excluded a
setting when its per-plant error was larger than that of the fixed radius in at least \calConfidence\,\%
of the samples (\calAllowedConfigurations{} of \calConfigurations{} settings remained). Among those we
chose the one with the smallest national error, the mean of the JMP error (region left out) and the
SDG 6.3.1 error (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-catchments}e). The national error falls with every
larger cap while the per-plant error rises, so the choice is the largest cap the catchments allow. The
chosen setting is $r_{\mathrm{cap}}=\calCap$\,km, $a=\calRuleA$ and $b=\calRuleB$, with national
scale limits \calScaleChosen. Repeating the choice with one reference country left out at a time gave
the same scale limits in \calLeaveOneOutSameLimits{} of \calLeaveOneOutRuns{} cases and caps between
\calLeaveOneOutCapLow{} and \calLeaveOneOutCapHigh\,km: the scale limits are robust, the cap less so.

\paragraph{Results.} Leaving each region out, the rule reduced the mean absolute error of the served
share against JMP from \calErrFixedJmp{} to \calErrRuleJmp{} percentage points (population-weighted:
from \calErrFixedJmpWeighted{} to \calErrRuleJmpWeighted), and against SDG 6.3.1, which was not fitted,
from \calErrFixedSdg{} to \calErrRuleSdg{} points (\calSdgCountries{} countries; Extended Data
Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-service-radius}c--e). Against Eurostat and OECD the error fell from
\calErrFixedEurostat{} to \calErrRuleEurostat{} (\calEurostatCountries{} countries) and from
\calErrFixedOecd{} to \calErrRuleOecd{} points (\calOecdCountries). The service areas cover fewer
people than these statistics report (mean difference \calBiasRuleSdg, \calBiasRuleEurostat{} and
\calBiasRuleOecd{} points), consistent with small plants missing from our list. Per plant, the chosen
setting is slightly worse than the fixed radius on average (typical factor \calPlantErrChosen{}
against \calPlantErrFixed; worse in \calShareWorse\,\% of the bootstrap samples), better in the
Netherlands (CBS counts \calTypicalNlCbsChosen{} against \calTypicalNlCbsFixed; purification units
\calTypicalNlUnitsChosen{} against \calTypicalNlUnitsFixed), about the same in the United States
(\calTypicalUsSewershedsChosen{} against \calTypicalUsSewershedsFixed) and worse in Great Britain
(\calTypicalGbCatchmentsChosen{} against \calTypicalGbCatchmentsFixed) and Ireland
(\calTypicalIeAgglomerationsChosen{} against \calTypicalIeAgglomerationsFixed; Extended Data
Fig.~\ref{fig:ed-catchments}a--d). The totals moved closer to the references in the United States
(predicted over reference \calSumRatioUsSewershedsFixed{} to \calSumRatioUsSewershedsChosen) and
the Netherlands (\calSumRatioNlCbsFixed{} to \calSumRatioNlCbsChosen); in Ireland the service areas
hold more people than the agglomeration polygons (\calSumRatioIeAgglomerationsFixed{} to
\calSumRatioIeAgglomerationsChosen). In Mexico, whose census reports the
dwellings with drainage (sewer or septic tank) for \calDistrictsMxIter{} municipalities, the service
areas hold \calServedMxIter\,\% of the population against \calDrainageMxIter\,\% of dwellings with
drainage, which is an upper bound for the population connected to a sewer.
